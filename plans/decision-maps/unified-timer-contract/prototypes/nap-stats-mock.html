Nap statistics: what gets added to the Sleep Summary

Everything on the screen today stays exactly where it is. Two things get added: one card for how much the baby naps on a day he naps, and one panel that breaks the day into its individual naps. Both appear in Statistics › Sleep › Summary and in the Sleep Patterns tab, because both render the same component.

A

Avg Nap Time

Total daytime nap seconds in the range, divided by the number of days in that range that hold at least one nap. A day where only the overnight was logged is not counted, and neither is a day with nothing logged.

avgNapTimePerNappingDay = sum(napSeconds over range) ÷ count(days where napSeconds > 0)

The subtitle carries the divisor — 5 of 7 — so the number is never read against a week it was not computed over.

It sits below the existing cards rather than replacing one. Its half-width slot mirrors how Avg Bedtime already renders alone when there is no bedtime to show.

B

Nap Schedule

Naps are numbered in the order they start within the sleep-day. Each row averages that slot over the days the slot actually happened — never over the whole range — and the day count under the label says how many that was.

slot n = the nth nap started that sleep-day length[n] = sum(duration of nap n) ÷ occ[n] starts[n] = circular mean of start times ← circularTimeMean() occ[n] = days where an nth nap exists

Start time uses the same circular mean the bedtime average already uses, so times straddling an hour boundary average correctly.

The panel goes below Averages, so the two existing per-nap figures keep their place and the breakdown reads as detail underneath them.

C

A slot earns a row when it clears both tests

It happened at least 3 times, and on at least 30% of the napping days in the range. Below either line the row does not render, so a rare fourth nap never reads as part of the routine.

Two naps a day, but only 3 napping days logged and the second nap fell on 2 of them. One row survives. When no slot clears both tests the whole panel is not rendered, the same way the Daily Sleep chart already hides itself with no bars.

What this does not change

  • Avg Total Sleep keeps its own divisor. It still divides by days with any sleep logged. Only the new card divides by napping days, so the two cards deliberately sit on different bases and the subtitle is what tells them apart.
  • Avg Naps/Day and Avg Nap Duration stay exactly as they are, in the Averages panel, computed the way they are computed today.
  • Nap versus night classification is untouched. Both additions read the same classifySleepByTimeRange result the rest of the screen already reads.
  • The newborn screen is unaffected in shape. Newborns already hide Avg Night Sleep and the split bar; the Nap Schedule panel simply falls away on its own once no slot is stable enough to clear the tests.

Still open

Worth a decision before this is planned.

  • The card's label. Avg Nap Time sits one line away from Avg Nap Duration, and the two mean different things — total per day versus length of one nap. Either the new label or the old one may need to change.
  • A skipped morning nap shifts the numbering. On a day the first nap does not happen, the midday nap becomes Nap 1 and pulls that row's average start time later. Accepted for now; the per-row day count is what makes the sample visible.
  • How far the numbering runs. Nothing caps the slot count today, so a newborn's 6-nap day would render 6 rows if each cleared the tests.
